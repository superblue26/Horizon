### [Henning Larsen Converts an 1883 Sawmill into a Marine Insurer’s Workplace](https://archinect.com/news/article/150576735/henning-larsen-transforms-1883-sawmill-into-marine-workplace) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Archinect \(海外建筑职场与行业\) · Nov 26, 15:33

**「Context」** Adaptive reuse of industrial heritage often has to reconcile preservation with contemporary workplace needs. The Strømsbu Sawmill case is notable because it pairs restoration of a waterfront timber and maritime site with material circularity and ecology-focused landscape design.

**「Project Approach」** Henning Larsen and Ramboll converted the 1883 Strømsbu Sawmill in Arendal, Norway, into a workplace for marine insurer Gard. The scheme restores the existing timber warehouse and sawmill, reconstructs a previously demolished intermediate building, and uses an extension plus a connecting structure to form three distinct volumes. Recovered natural stone from the original framework and foundations is reused in new walls, landscape features and drainage; timber logs from the former sawmill become terraces and stairs; and reclaimed tiles reference the site’s historic industrial floors. Working with ecologists at Ramboll, the landscape incorporates native vegetation, sand beds, bee hotels, fallen timber, coastal grasses, meadows and nectar-producing plants to support insects, birds and local butterfly populations.

**「Takeaway」** For architects, the project shows that adaptive reuse can integrate heritage, salvaged materials and biodiversity rather than treating them as separate add-ons. It remains a brief project showcase, however, and does not detail BIM workflows or direct career and market implications.

### [Zunli Tea House: Constraint-Led Renewal in Suzhou&\#x27;s Gusu District](https://www.archdaily.com/1188938/zunli-tea-house-out-of-office-design) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · ArchDaily · Nov 26, 12:00

**「Background」** Zunli Tea House is a small adaptive-reuse project in an old dwelling in Suzhou&\#x27;s historic Gusu district, where the client wanted the intimacy and shifting views of a classical Chinese garden brought into daily domestic life. The challenge was not a blank interior but a fragile historic building shaped by a modest budget, tenancy-related restrictions and limited scope for structural intervention.

**「Design Response」** OUT OF OFFICE DESIGN kept the original room arrangement, accepted that reinforcement opportunities were limited, and avoided cutting or chasing the walls for services. Electrical conduits were therefore run surface-mounted. With major architectural moves constrained, furniture, planting and finishes became the main tools for reshaping the interior and supporting the tea-house atmosphere.

**「Practical Takeaway」** For architects working in constrained heritage interiors, the project shows the value of designing around retained fabric rather than forcing conventional hidden-services upgrades. Its practical lesson is to treat budget, tenancy rules and structural fragility as early design parameters that can shape a coherent low-intervention scheme.

### [Canada issues 600 STEM Express Entry invitations at CRS 547](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-invites-600-stem-workers-to-apply-for-permanent-residence-through-express-entry-1182888.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 15:13

**「Context」** Canada’s Express Entry system is operating under a revised Comprehensive Ranking System that took effect on Nov. 3, 2026, with recent draws heavily weighted toward provincial nominees, Canadian Experience Class candidates, and French speakers. The new STEM round is the first such draw in more than two and a half years, after the previous STEM selection on April 11, 2024, issued 4,500 invitations at a CRS cutoff of 491.

**「Policy details」** On Nov. 26, IRCC issued 600 Invitations to Apply in a STEM occupations draw, requiring a minimum CRS score of 547 and an Express Entry profile created before 4:52:51 p.m. UTC on Dec. 2, 2025. IRCC has now held 68 Express Entry draws in 2026 and issued 158,008 ITAs, with Canadian Experience Class, French-language proficiency, and Provincial Nominee Program draws accounting for most invitations. Under the new Labour Market Integration Factor, candidates can earn the higher of additional points for Canadian work experience or a qualifying high-wage job offer; 186 occupations are classified as high-wage by NOC, not salary, with work-experience bonuses of 20, 40, or 70 points and job-offer bonuses of 50, 70, or 100 points, capped at 150 points \(140 with a spouse or common-law partner\). Qualifying job offers no longer require an LMIA, and a temporary policy effective Nov. 3 allows eligible FSWP/FSTP candidates invited on or after that date to be exempt from the LMIA requirement; as of Nov. 17, NOC 41200 can count non-wage Canadian funding, while NOC 41201 was removed from the Researchers with Canadian work experience category.

**「Practical impact and vocabulary」** For prospective STEM applicants, the 547 cutoff and small 600-ITA round show that category eligibility alone is unlikely to be enough; Canadian work experience, a provincial nomination, or French proficiency is likely to be more decisive. Architects and BIM/AEC professionals should confirm that their NOC falls within the STEM category and plan for a much higher score than the prior 491 STEM cutoff.

### [Canada to Restrict Low-Wage TFWP to In-Demand Occupations from March 2027](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-moves-to-restrict-low-wage-tfwp-to-in-demand-occupations-1182920.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 22:14

**「Background」** Canada’s low-wage Temporary Foreign Worker Program is being tightened after a September 2024 moratorium in census metropolitan areas \(CMAs\) with unemployment of 6% or higher and a November 2024 increase in the low-wage threshold to 120% of the provincial or territorial median wage. The 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan also sets TFWP admissions at 50,000 in 2027, down from an annual target of 82,000 as of fall 2025, while Prime Minister Mark Carney has said reforms should target strategic sectors and regional needs.

**「Policy Design」** ESDC announced on Nov. 6, 2026 that from March 1, 2027, opted-in provinces and territories will process low-wage Labour Market Impact Assessments \(LMIAs\) only for whitelisted occupations when the job is in a CMA and pays less than 120% of the provincial or territorial median wage. The whitelist will be a base list of Canadian Occupational Projection System \(COPS\) occupations—ESDC said these would be occupations experiencing labour shortages but did not specify whether recent conditions or future projections, or moderate versus strong shortages, would govern inclusion—plus provincial or territorial additions and minus removals; in population centres of large CMAs \(over 250,000\), the existing moratorium still applies, so no low-wage LMIA is processed where unemployment is 6% or higher unless a priority-sector exemption applies. In smaller CMAs \(100,000–250,000\) and outside population centres within large CMAs, the whitelist will supersede the unemployment-based moratorium from March 1, 2027; until then, ESDC will accept low-wage LMIAs for all occupations in all regions subject to the existing moratorium. ESDC also extended maximum low-wage LMIA duration to two years from one on Nov. 6, 2026; current low-wage thresholds are $36.92/hour in Ontario and $38.40/hour in British Columbia, and 30 CMAs remain under the moratorium through Jan. 7, 2027.

**「Takeaway」** For employers and applicants, the low-wage route will depend on occupation whitelisting, CMA size, local unemployment, and priority-sector status, not just the wage threshold. Architects and BIM/AEC professionals paid above the 120% threshold are outside the core low-wage restriction, but LMIA-dependent hires should confirm provincial opt-in and occupation eligibility before planning.

### [Essay argues mainstream languages should be designed for formal verification](https://blainehansen.me/post/mainstream-verifiable-language/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The essay argues that a mainstream programming language should be designed from the start for formal verifiability, so that machine-checkable correctness proofs become practical for ordinary developers rather than a specialist add-on. It is an opinion piece, not an announcement: no language, version, implementation or measured result is presented. Commenters situate the argument against existing verification-oriented systems — Dafny, SPARK Ada, Why3, Verus, Frama-C and TLA+ — which target different languages and styles of verification, and several note that LLMs are now making such tools more approachable for non-specialists. The discussion also stresses a limit of the premise: as one commenter quotes Knuth, a proof of correctness does not establish that the program does what was actually wanted.

hackernews · todsacerdoti · Nov 27, 05:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50663089)

**「Background」** Formal verification means proving that code satisfies a written specification rather than testing it, and the tools that do this today are mostly tied to particular languages and ecosystems — Dafny, Verus for Rust, SPARK for Ada, Frama-C for C, and TLA+ for design-level specifications. Studies of Dafny and SPARK adoption have examined how auto-active verification changes day-to-day software development and why these tools remain confined to specialised and safety-critical work. A benchmark of &quot;vericoding&quot; has also assembled 12,504 formal specifications in Dafny, Verus and Lean to test whether LLM-generated code can come with proofs rather than just plausible output.

**「Practical path for teams」** Teams that want machine-checked correctness today face a choice between adopting a verification-oriented language and proving existing code: commenters cite Dafny, SPARK Ada, Verus \(SMT-based verification for Rust\), Frama-C and TLA+, while one commenter&\#x27;s ProgLib project instead targets Lean proofs over Python, C++, Rust and SystemVerilog via formalized semantics. The binding constraint is specification rather than proof checking — as one 2026 verification guide puts it, LLM-written proofs that fail are simply rejected by the checker, but &quot;proving code correct only helps if you specified the right thing, and that part is still on you.&quot;

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters question whether verification can be designed into mainstream adoption: porcoda argues that mainstreamness comes from adoption and suggests studying why Dafny, SPARK Ada, Why3, Verus, Frama-C and TLA+ did not become mainstream, while thomasahle contends a new verifiable language is unnecessary if existing languages are given formalized semantics, citing his ProgLib project for proving properties of Python, C++, Rust and SystemVerilog code in Lean. hresvelgr, invoking Knuth&\#x27;s quip about proved-but-untried code, reports that writing TLA+ specs has not materially improved some teams&\#x27; software quality or reliability.
