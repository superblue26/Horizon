### [Henning Larsen Converts an 1883 Norwegian Sawmill into a Marine Insurer&\#x27;s Workplace](https://archinect.com/news/article/150576735/henning-larsen-transforms-1883-sawmill-into-marine-workplace) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Archinect \(海外建筑职场与行业\) · Nov 26, 15:33

**「Context」** Across Europe, former industrial waterfronts are increasingly valued less as development land than as irreplaceable civic and material assets, which puts pressure on architects to justify demolition. Norway&\#x27;s coastal timber and maritime economy left behind exactly this kind of stock: large, robust structures on ecologically sensitive shoreline. The Strømsbu Steam Saw and Planing Mill in Arendal, founded in 1883, is a case in point — over its life it operated as a sawmill, timber outlet, warehouse and car repair shop before falling into partial disuse, with an intermediate building already lost. Turning such a site into a contemporary corporate office raises a dual design problem: how to meet workplace standards without erasing industrial character, and how to build on rocky coastal terrain without flattening its habitats.

**「Approach」** Henning Larsen, working with engineer Ramboll, completed the conversion for marine insurer Gard by treating the existing fabric as the primary material bank rather than as a constraint. The scheme restores the original timber warehouse and sawmill and reconstructs the previously demolished intermediate building; together with a new extension to the warehouse and a connecting structure, these establish three distinct volumes across the site. Reuse is written into the construction logic: natural stone recovered from the original framework and foundations reappears in new walls, landscape features and a drainage system, while timber logs from the former sawmill become terraces and stairs, and reclaimed tiles echo the site&\#x27;s historic industrial floors. The landscape was developed alongside Ramboll ecologists and responds to the rocky coastal terrain with native vegetation and wildlife habitat — sand beds, bee hotels and fallen timber for nesting pollinators, plus coastal grasses, meadows and nectar-producing plants intended to support local butterfly populations.

**「Implication」** The project shows that heritage conversion can be organised around a salvage inventory — stone, logs, tiles mapped before demolition — turning restoration into a materials strategy rather than a finish. For architects and BIM practitioners, it also argues for pairing the building model with an ecological one, so habitat measures are designed as part of the scheme and not appended afterwards.

**「Context」** Scotland&\#x27;s RIAS Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland Award, named after architect Andrew Doolan who died in 2004, is given annually to buildings of any typology. **「Approach」** Bennetts Associates converted a Category A-listed Victorian infirmary in Edinburgh, originally designed by Scottish architect David Bryce, into the Edinburgh Futures Institute for the University of Edinburgh. ### [Canada to Restrict Low-Wage TFWP to In-Demand Occupations from March 2027](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-moves-to-restrict-low-wage-tfwp-to-in-demand-occupations-1182920.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 22:14

**「Background」** Canada&\#x27;s Temporary Foreign Worker Program has been tightening its low-wage stream for several years: since September 2024, low-wage LMIAs have been refused in census metropolitan areas with unemployment of 6% or higher, and in November 2024 the low-wage threshold was raised from the provincial median to 120% of it. Pressure to narrow the programme further is reinforced by the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan, which sets TFWP admissions at 50,000 for 2027, down from an annual target of 82,000 as of autumn 2025, with Prime Minister Mark Carney describing the goal as a &quot;focused approach&quot; targeting strategic sectors and regional needs.

**「The New Rules」** ESDC announced on Nov. 6, 2026 that, effective March 1, 2027, opting-in provinces and territories will process low-wage LMIAs only for whitelisted occupations — meaning jobs located within CMAs and paying less than 120% of the provincial or territorial median wage. Each whitelist will combine a base list drawn from the Canadian Occupational Projection System \(COPS\) with occupations provinces add or remove; ESDC told CIC News the base covers &quot;occupations experiencing labour shortages&quot; but did not clarify whether it draws on 2023–2025 conditions, 2026–2035 projections, or which shortage intensity qualifies. In large CMAs \(population above 250,000\) the existing 6%-unemployment moratorium continues alongside the whitelist, with priority-sector exemptions such as agriculture, hospitals and construction, whereas in CMAs of 100,000–250,000 and areas outside population centres the whitelist supersedes the moratorium. ESDC also extended the maximum low-wage LMIA duration to two years from one; current low-wage thresholds stand at $36.92 per hour in Ontario and $38.40 in British Columbia, and 30 CMAs remain under the moratorium through Jan. 7, 2027.

**「Takeaway」** The low-wage sponsored route is narrowing to occupations a province explicitly treats as in shortage, so employer-driven applications will hinge on occupation choice, wage level and CMA location. Higher-wage roles and LMIA-exempt International Mobility Program permits — 170,000 planned for 2027 — sit outside these restrictions, which is where most architects and BIM/AEC professionals would fall.

### [DNA Kits and Canadian Citizenship by Descent: Leads, Not Proof](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/a-dna-kit-can-point-you-to-a-canadian-ancestor-is-it-enough-to-claim-citizenship-by-descent-1182909.html) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 18:23

**「Context」** Interest in Canadian citizenship by descent has grown among Americans who may already be citizens without knowing it; CIC News has reported that an estimated three million people in New England alone may be eligible. Since Bill C-3 took effect on 15 December 2025, Canadian citizenship can pass down a direct family line with no generational limit for people born before that date, and Canadian law already treats qualifying individuals as citizens from birth — they apply only for proof, then a certificate and passport. The practical bottleneck is often genealogical: applicants hit a wall when their line runs through relatives nobody living can name.

**「What the Rules Allow」** Consumer kits from AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage, FamilyTreeDNA and Living DNA offer relative matching that can surface unknown cousins, repeated surnames \(including French &quot;dit names&quot;\), named parishes and birth dates pointing to the archive that holds an official record; results typically arrive in four to eight weeks. These matches are leads only: they reach just people who tested with the same company and opted into matching, French Canadian lines descending from roughly 8,500 early settlers overlap too heavily for a kit to resolve every connection, and IRCC does not accept self-arranged DNA results — it may request an accredited-lab test after application solely to confirm a parent–child link across one generation. Instead, applicants must document every generation back to the Canadian anchor ancestor, with primary records supported by secondary ones and every document obtained from the office that created or holds it, since genealogy-site screenshots do not count on their own. Quebec adds a further constraint: IRCC will not accept Quebec birth or marriage certificates issued before 1 January 1994, so certified copies of parish baptism entries from Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec are used for older births. For children born abroad on or after 15 December 2025, a Canadian parent who was also born abroad needs 1,095 days \(three years\) of physical presence in Canada before the birth.

**「Practical Impact」** For architects, BIM/AEC professionals and other skilled workers, confirmed citizenship by descent can remove the need for a Canadian work permit entirely, but a DNA match is a research shortcut rather than a legal basis for a claim. Lines running through an ancestor born before 1947 or in Newfoundland before 1 April 1949, through relatives who later became American, or through deceased claimants are complex enough that the source recommends legal review of documents before applying.
