rss · Archinect \(海外建筑职场与行业\) · Nov 26, 15:33

**「Context」** The Strømsbu Sawmill in Arendal, Norway, dates to 1883 and reflects the country’s timber and maritime industries. Henning Larsen and Ramboll’s conversion for marine insurer Gard shows how a waterfront industrial complex can be adapted for contemporary office use while retaining its historic character.

**「Design and Delivery」** The scheme restores the timber warehouse and sawmill and reconstructs a previously demolished intermediate building; an extension and connector create three distinct volumes. **「Practical Takeaway」** The project demonstrates that adaptive reuse can pair heritage retention with material recovery and ecology-led landscape design, rather than treating sustainability as a separate layer. For architects, early integration of structural reuse, reclaimed materials and ecological habitats can make a constrained waterfront site more distinctive and environmentally responsive.

rss · Dezeen Architecture · Nov 27, 00:01

**「A Vacant Victorian Landmark and a Reuse-Focused Prize」** Scotland holds a large stock of Victorian institutional buildings whose original uses have ended, and the Edinburgh Futures Institute site is a stark example: the Category A-listed infirmary, originally designed by Scottish architect David Bryce in the Scottish Baronial manner, was vacated in 2003 and subsequently at risk of dereliction. The RIAS Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland Award, an annual prize named after Scottish architect Andrew Doolan, who passed away in 2004, recognises buildings of all typologies, and in 2026 all three finalists were adaptive reuse schemes — the Tarlair Outdoor Pool Pavilion by Studio Octopi and Paisley Central Library by Collective Architecture alongside the winner. That shortlist signals how strongly the Scottish awards conversation has shifted toward reworking existing fabric rather than new-build. Previous winners include Union Terrace Gardens by Stallan-Brand and LDA Design and the Burrell Collection museum by John McAslan + Partners.

**「Restoring Bryce&\#x27;s Diagram While Inserting New Program」** Bennetts Associates refurbished the listed structure for the University of Edinburgh, restoring existing fabric and reopening an original clock tower and public plaza. Original wards and 150-metre-long corridors were brought back into use, preserving the long, repetitive planning logic of the infirmary. Across five floors the studio added flexible teaching and work zones plus new public areas, including exhibition space and a 450-seat event hall below ground. RIAS president Libby Heathcote, associate director at Reiach and Hall Architects, said the jury valued the project&\#x27;s &quot;civic generosity, scale and ambition,&quot; describing the balance between &quot;loyalty to David Bryce&\#x27;s diagram&quot; and &quot;bold new interventions&quot; as a remarkable achievement that blends &quot;historic Scottish Baronial architecture with modern innovation.&quot; She called the result &quot;a modern, welcoming, clever, and stimulating place that people want to work in and return to.&quot; The source is an award announcement and does not detail structural, servicing or digital-modelling methods.

**「What the Win Means in Practice」** For architects and BIM professionals, the case shows that surveying and documenting a protected envelope precisely enough to slot new teaching, exhibition and 450-seat event spaces into it is where the design value now sits. Heritage-sensitive reuse, judged on civic generosity rather than novelty, is a credible route to national recognition.

### [Canada&\#x27;s 600-ITA STEM Express Entry Draw and Revised CRS](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-invites-600-stem-workers-to-apply-for-permanent-residence-through-express-entry-1182888.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 15:13

**「Background」** Canada&\#x27;s Express Entry system ranks skilled workers through the Comprehensive Ranking System \(CRS\) and uses category-based draws to target labour gaps, and STEM workers had not been selected since April 11, 2024, while 2026 draws have otherwise emphasized provincial nominees and Canadian-experience candidates.

**「Policy details」** On Nov. 26, 2026, IRCC issued 600 STEM Invitations to Apply at a CRS cutoff of 547; candidates also had to have created an Express Entry profile before 4:52:51 p.m. UTC on Dec. 2, 2025. The round was the first STEM draw in more than two and a half years, versus 4,500 ITAs and a 491 cutoff on Apr. 11, 2024, and brought the year to 68 Express Entry draws and 158,008 ITAs. Effective Nov. 3, 2026, the new Labour Market Integration Factor awards the higher of two scores: at least one year of Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation adds 20, 40, or 70 points, within a 150-point cap \(140 with a spouse\), while a qualifying job offer adds 50, 70, or 100 points and no longer requires an LMIA. IRCC identifies 186 high-wage occupations by NOC code, not salary, with three levels based on whether the national median wage is at least 1.3, 1.5, or two times the average median wage. A temporary policy in force from Nov. 3 lets officers exempt eligible candidates from the LMIA requirement for job offers used for FSWP/FSTP eligibility, and as of Nov. 17, NOC 41200 professors and lecturers may count non-wage Canadian funding such as stipends, grants, bursaries, and scholarships, while NOC 41201 teaching/research assistants were removed from the Researchers with Canadian work experience category.

**「Practical impact」** STEM candidates still need very high CRS scores, so architects, BIM/AEC professionals, and other skilled workers should treat the new high-wage work-experience and job-offer points as a narrow supplement rather than a guaranteed route. The LMIA waiver and NOC-specific updates may help eligible candidates, but occupational classification and exact wage-level rules will determine the benefit.

### [Canada to Restrict Low-Wage TFWP to In-Demand Occupations Starting 2027](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-moves-to-restrict-low-wage-tfwp-to-in-demand-occupations-1182920.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 22:14

**「Background」** Canada’s Temporary Foreign Worker Program has been tightening its low-wage stream amid labour-market and admission pressures. ESDC introduced a low-wage moratorium in September 2024 and raised the low-wage threshold to 120% of the provincial or territorial median wage in November 2024, while the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan cuts planned TFWP admissions to 50,000 in 2027 from an 82,000 annual target as of fall 2025.

**「Policy Changes」** ESDC announced on Nov. 6, 2026 that, starting March 1, 2027, opting-in provinces and territories will process low-wage LMIAs only for whitelisted occupations when the job is in a CMA and pays less than 120% of the provincial or territorial median wage. Whitelists will combine COPS occupations with provincial or territorial additions and removals; ESDC told CIC News on Nov. 17 that the base list relates to labour-shortage occupations but did not clarify which COPS years, shortage levels, or seasonality would be used. In large CMAs \(over 250,000 people\), the existing moratorium remains: no low-wage LMIA processing if unemployment is 6% or higher, even for whitelisted occupations, unless a priority-sector exemption applies, while in smaller CMAs \(100,000–250,000\) and areas outside population centres in large CMAs the whitelist supersedes the unemployment-based moratorium. Until March 1, 2027, low-wage LMIAs remain accepted subject to the existing moratorium, with 30 CMAs affected through Jan. 7, 2027; current low-wage thresholds are $36.92/hour in Ontario and $38.40/hour in British Columbia, and ESDC extended low-wage LMIA maximum duration to two years from one on Nov. 6, 2026.

**「Practical Impact」** Prospective low-wage TFWP applicants will face narrower access because provincial whitelists, CMA size, unemployment rates, and wage thresholds will determine whether an LMIA can be processed. Architects, BIM/AEC professionals and other skilled workers should check whether their job falls in the low-wage stream and affected CMA; roles above the low-wage threshold and IMP routes sit outside this specific restriction.

### [DNA Kits and Canadian Citizenship by Descent: Leads, Not Proof](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/a-dna-kit-can-point-you-to-a-canadian-ancestor-is-it-enough-to-claim-citizenship-by-descent-1182909.html) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 18:23

**「Background」** Canada&\#x27;s citizenship-by-descent framework changed when Bill C-3 took effect on 15 December 2025, removing the generational limit on passing citizenship down a direct family line for people born before that date. CIC News reports that millions of Americans may already be Canadian citizens without knowing it — an estimated three million in New England alone — and that the hardest step for many is simply documenting an ancestor when the line runs through relatives the family never knew.

**「What the Rules Actually Require」** Consumer DNA kits \(AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage, FamilyTreeDNA, Living DNA\) can generate leads — cousin matches, repeated surnames, parish or town names — but results take four to eight weeks and cannot prove a claim: IRCC does not accept self-arranged DNA results. IRCC uses DNA only after an application is filed, when it may request an accredited-lab test confirming a parent–child link; that covers a single generation, so the paper trail back to the Canadian anchor ancestor is still required. Evidence is sorted into primary and secondary tiers, with secondary records supporting rather than replacing a primary document, and every record must come from the office that created or holds it — genealogy-site screenshots do not count on their own. Quebec carries an extra rule: IRCC will not accept Quebec birth or marriage certificates issued before 1 January 1994, so Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec issues a certified copy of the parish baptism entry instead; a successful application yields a citizenship certificate, which supports a passport application, while children born abroad on or after 15 December 2025 face different rules, including 1,095 days of parental physical presence in Canada where the Canadian parent was also born abroad.

**「Takeaway」** For prospective applicants, a DNA kit is a research accelerator rather than evidence: it points to the cousins, spellings and parishes that lead to official records, which must then be ordered generation by generation. This pathway concerns proof of citizenship rather than work visas, so skilled professionals should treat it as a separate track from employer-sponsored or skilled migration routes. ### [Essay argues for a formally verifiable mainstream programming language](https://blainehansen.me/post/mainstream-verifiable-language/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An essay argues that formal verification should be built into a mainstream programming language rather than remain confined to specialist languages and tools. It presents a language-design argument rather than a release or implementation; the Hacker News discussion focuses on existing verification systems and why they have not achieved mainstream adoption.

hackernews · todsacerdoti · Nov 27, 05:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50663089)

**「Background」** Formal verification means proving that code satisfies a written specification, typically through preconditions, postconditions, loop invariants, and termination conditions that a static verifier checks. Dafny is a long-established example of a verification-aware language: it records specifications natively, ships a static program verifier, and compiles to C\#, Java, JavaScript, Go, and Python, with IDE feedback from a language server. The essay&\#x27;s premise is that capabilities of this kind have remained in specialized tools rather than being built into a mainstream language.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters pushed back from different angles: porcoda argued that “mainstreamness comes from adoption” and that the useful question is why Dafny, SPARK Ada, Why3, Verus, Frama-C, and TLA+ stayed niche, while hresvelgr warned that formal correctness does not guarantee the software does what users want. thomasahle proposed formalizing existing languages&\#x27; semantics and proving properties in Lean via ProgLib rather than migrating code to a new verifiable language.
