### [Architectural League Names 25 Winners of the 2026 Independent Projects Grants](https://archinect.com/news/bustler/11223/the-architectural-league-awards-25-recipients-of-2026-independent-projects-grants) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Archinect \(海外建筑职场与行业\) · Nov 26, 16:52

**「Context」** The Architectural League of New York, together with the New York State Council on the Arts, has announced the 2026 cohort of its Independent Projects grants — a program that funds self-directed design research rather than built commissions. For architects and designers, this matters because the funding landscape for speculative, research-based, or community-embedded work sits largely outside conventional office employment: micro-grants of this size rarely cover a full project, but they provide the seed capital and institutional recognition that let an individual practitioner pursue an idea without a client. The program is explicitly statewide in scope, so it also signals how cultural funding is being distributed beyond New York City into upstate design communities.

**「The 2026 Cohort」** Twenty-five proposals were selected from a pool of 202 applicants, and each recipient receives a $10,000 grant. The list spans a deliberately wide range of formats and disciplines: publications and archival research \(Sonya Abrego&\#x27;s study of Sweet-Orr &amp; Co. work clothes; Daniel Jonas Roche and Brad Isnard&\#x27;s history of Herman Jessor and the limited-equity cooperative movement in New York City, 1926–1973\), interior and material investigations \(Nicola Cortese&\#x27;s Reassembled Interiors; Sara Elise&\#x27;s Threshold: A Grammar of Sensory Design\), and public-space and urban-ecology propositions such as Floating Garden, Garden of Leftover Space by Matthew Sprague, Sebastijan Jemec and Aaron Sexton, and A Depaving Action Plan by Gabriel Vergara, Amelyn Ng and Christine Giorgio. Several projects engage civic infrastructure and equity directly, including Andrea Johnson&\#x27;s participatory design work around the decommissioning of 287 Maspeth and a just energy transition, and Emma Silverblatt&\#x27;s research on value in manufactured home communities. Geographically the recipients cluster in New York City but extend to Ithaca \(Zhicheng Xu&\#x27;s Architecture of Second Growth, Rosita Palladino&\#x27;s Dancing the Giglio, and others\) and Syracuse \(Nazanin Ghaffari&\#x27;s reading of plantation landscapes\).

**「Takeaway」** For independent practitioners, the lesson is structural rather than stylistic: modest $10,000 awards attached to a named institution can convert an unfunded research interest into a citable, portfolio-defining project. Applicants should note the program&\#x27;s evident appetite for work that pairs archival or community research with a clear public audience and a specific New York State location.

### [Henning Larsen Converts an 1883 Norwegian Sawmill Into a Marine Insurer&\#x27;s Workplace](https://archinect.com/news/article/150576735/henning-larsen-transforms-1883-sawmill-into-marine-workplace) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Archinect \(海外建筑职场与行业\) · Nov 26, 15:33

**「Context」** Norway&\#x27;s coastal towns were shaped by timber and maritime trade, and many of their industrial buildings now sit idle on valuable waterfront land. The Strømsbu Steam Saw and Planing Mill in Arendal, founded in 1883, is one such site: over the decades it has operated as a sawmill, timber outlet, warehouse and car repair shop. Rather than clear the complex for new construction, the client and design team chose adaptive reuse, treating the existing structures and their embedded materials as the starting point for a contemporary office.

**「The Conversion」** Henning Larsen, working with engineering consultancy Ramboll, completed the transformation of the sawmill into a workplace for the marine insurer Gard. The scheme restores the timber warehouse and sawmill buildings and reconstructs a previously demolished intermediate building, while an extension to the warehouse and a connecting structure establish three distinct volumes across the site. Material reuse drives the detail: natural stone recovered from the original framework and foundations reappears in new walls, landscape features and a drainage system, timber logs from the former mill become terraces and stairs, and reclaimed tiles echo the site&\#x27;s historic industrial floors. The landscape, developed with Ramboll&\#x27;s ecologists in response to the rocky coastal terrain, adds native vegetation and habitats including sand beds, bee hotels and fallen timber for nesting insects and birds, with coastal grasses, meadows and nectar-producing plants intended to support local butterfly populations.

**「Practical Significance」** For architects, the project shows how a heritage industrial shell can be re-read as a materials bank, with stone, timber and tiles downgraded from waste to specification. It also demonstrates that biodiversity goals can be designed into a commercial workplace brief when ecologists join the team early.

### [Canada resumes STEM Express Entry draws with 600 ITAs at CRS 547](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-invites-600-stem-workers-to-apply-for-permanent-residence-through-express-entry-1182888.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 15:13

**「Background」** Canada’s Express Entry system is operating under a revised Comprehensive Ranking System that took effect on Nov. 3, 2026, adding new weight for high-wage Canadian work experience and job offers. According to CIC News, IRCC had not held a STEM-specific draw for more than two and a half years, while its 2026 selections concentrated on provincial nominees, Canadian Experience Class candidates and French-language speakers.

**「Policy details」** IRCC issued 600 Invitations to Apply to STEM candidates on Nov. 26, 2026, at a minimum CRS score of 547; candidates also needed a profile created before 4:52:51 p.m. UTC on Dec. 2, 2025. The previous STEM draw, on Apr. 11, 2024, issued 4,500 ITAs at a CRS cut-off of 491, and the Nov. 26 round was IRCC’s 68th Express Entry draw of 2026, bringing the year’s total to 158,008 ITAs. Under the new Labour Market Integration Factor, candidates in 186 NOC-based occupations—classified by whether their national median wage is at least 1.3, 1.5 or two times the workforce average median, not by the candidate’s salary—can earn 20, 40 or 70 additional points for at least one year of Canadian work experience, or 50, 70 or 100 points for a qualifying job offer, with no LMIA required; the factor awards the higher of the two scores and caps combined experience points at 150 \(140 with a spouse or common-law partner\). A temporary policy allows LMIA exemptions for job offers used for FSWP/FSTP eligibility for candidates invited on or after Nov. 3, and as of Nov. 17, NOC 41200 university professors and lecturers may count non-wage funding from a Canadian entity, such as stipends, grants, bursaries and scholarships, while NOC 41201 post-secondary teaching and research assistants were removed from the Researchers with Canadian work experience category.

**「Implications」** For prospective applicants, including architects and BIM/AEC professionals who qualify under a STEM-eligible NOC, the draw signals that category eligibility alone is not enough: a CRS at or above 547 in this round and strong human-capital or French-language points remain decisive. The new high-wage factor makes documented Canadian experience or a qualifying job offer more valuable, even without an LMIA, so early NOC and wage-level checks matter.

### [Canada to Restrict Low-Wage TFWP to In-Demand Occupations from March 2027](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/canada-moves-to-restrict-low-wage-tfwp-to-in-demand-occupations-1182920.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 22:14

**「Background」** Canada has been tightening the low-wage stream of the Temporary Foreign Worker Program since it imposed a moratorium in September 2024 and, in November 2024, raised the low-wage threshold from the provincial or territorial median to 120% of it. With the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan cutting TFWP admissions to 50,000 in 2027 from an 82,000 annual target as of autumn 2025, Ottawa is now framing reform as a &quot;focused approach&quot; targeting strategic sectors and specific regional needs — a direction Prime Minister Mark Carney outlined in a September 2025 address.

**「Solution」** Employment and Social Development Canada&\#x27;s November 6, 2026 announcement sets March 1, 2027 as the start date: in provinces and territories that opt in, low-wage Labour Market Impact Assessments will be processed only for whitelisted occupations where the job sits inside a census metropolitan area and pays less than 120% of the provincial or territorial median wage. Each whitelist will be a base list of occupations from the Canadian Occupational Projection System, plus occupations the province or territory adds and minus those it removes — a system ESDC calls &quot;targeted access&quot;; the department told CIC News the base list reflects &quot;occupations experiencing labour shortages&quot; but did not specify whether it draws on 2023–2025 conditions or 2026–2035 projections, nor which shortage intensity or seasonality applies. In population centres of large CMAs \(over 250,000 people\) the existing moratorium survives alongside the whitelist, so no low-wage LMIA is processed where unemployment is 6% or higher unless a priority-sector exemption applies \(agriculture, hospitals and construction among them\), while in smaller CMAs \(100,000–250,000\) and outside population centres the whitelist replaces the unemployment-rate moratorium. Until March 2027 all occupations remain eligible subject to the current moratorium, which covers 30 CMAs through January 7, 2027; current low-wage thresholds are $36.92 per hour in Ontario and $38.40 per hour in British Columbia, and on November 6, 2026 ESDC extended maximum low-wage LMIA duration to two years from one.

**「Takeaway」** Employers and workers who depend on low-wage LMIAs in Canadian metro areas will face a narrower occupation list and continuing unemployment-rate bars from March 2027, so applicants — including architects and BIM/AEC professionals paid below the 120% threshold — should check their occupation against provincial whitelists and plan for longer or alternative pathways. Higher-wage LMIAs and LMIA-exempt International Mobility Program permits \(170,000 admissions planned for 2027\) fall outside these low-wage restrictions.

### [DNA Kits and Canadian Citizenship by Descent: Leads, Not Proof](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/11/a-dna-kit-can-point-you-to-a-canadian-ancestor-is-it-enough-to-claim-citizenship-by-descent-1182909.html) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 26, 18:23

**「Context」** Canada&\#x27;s citizenship-by-descent framework was long capped by a generational limit, leaving many people with Canadian ancestry—CIC News cites an estimated three million potentially eligible Americans in New England alone—unable to confirm a status the law already treated them as holding. Bill C-3 changed that calculus, shifting the practical bottleneck from legal eligibility to proving the family line with documents, which is where most applicants hit a wall.

**「The Rules and the Evidence」** Since Bill C-3 took effect on December 15, 2025, Canadian citizenship passes down a direct family line with no generational limit for people born before that date; for children born abroad on or after December 15, 2025 whose Canadian parent was also born abroad, that parent needs 1,095 days \(three years\) of physical presence in Canada before the birth. Consumer DNA kits from AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage, FamilyTreeDNA and Living DNA can surface cousin matches, original surnames and &quot;dit names,&quot; parishes, and birth dates, but IRCC does not accept self-arranged DNA results as evidence—it may only request a test from an accredited lab to confirm a parent-child link across one generation. Applicants must supply records for every generation showing who was whose child and that the anchor ancestor was Canadian, which IRCC sorts into primary and secondary tiers where secondary records support rather than replace a primary document, and every record must come from the office that created or holds it. Quebec adds a further constraint: IRCC will not accept Quebec birth or marriage certificates issued before January 1, 1994, so older births require a certified copy of the parish baptism entry from Bibliothèque et Archives nationales du Québec \(BAnQ\).

**「Practical Impact」** For architects, BIM/AEC professionals and other skilled workers, this is a parallel route to Canadian status for those with Canadian lineage—not a work-visa pathway—and it depends entirely on a documented paper trail rather than a DNA result. Treat matches as research leads, begin ordering civil and parish records early since kits take four to eight weeks, and expect complex lines \(ancestors born before 1947, Newfoundland before April 1, 1949, or relatives who later became American\) to warrant legal review.

**「Background」** British Columbia&\#x27;s Provincial Nominee Program continues to run dedicated Entrepreneur Immigration streams for foreign nationals who intend to launch, acquire or expand a business in the province, even as provinces work within a fixed federal nominations allocation. On Nov. 12, IRCC announced that the provinces have until March 31, 2027, to issue tens of thousands of additional nominations to in-Canada foreign nationals and their accompanying family members.

**「What the November 17 draw changed」** In its November 17 selection, the BCPNP issued no fewer than 13 invitations to apply: 12 under the Base Stream and fewer than five under the Regional Stream, both at a minimum score of 117. The Base Stream invitation count was the highest of any draw since June 30, and the cutoff score was one to two points lower than in the previous two selections; 2026 has now seen 12 entrepreneur draws with no fewer than 140 invitations, and no fewer than 7,614 invitations once Skills Immigration is included. Base Stream candidates need a post-secondary credential, CLB 4, three years of business owner-manager experience in the past 10 years, $600,000 in personal net worth, a $200,000 investment, 33.33% ownership and a 115/200 registration score, while the Regional Stream — for new businesses outside the Metro Vancouver Regional District — requires three years of experience in the past five, $300,000 net worth, $100,000 investment, 51% ownership, a community referral, an exploratory visit and a 105/200 score. Approved applicants receive a work permit support letter and, after fulfilling their business performance agreement, a nomination to apply to IRCC for permanent residence, though BC has not disclosed how many invitations have converted into nominations or how much of its 6,254-nomination 2026 allocation remains.

**「Takeaway」** Because these are business-owner streams rather than employment-based ones, they offer little direct pathway for architects, BIM/AEC professionals or other skilled workers, who rely instead on the province&\#x27;s Skills Immigration draws — reported here only in aggregate, with nomination uptake undisclosed. hackernews · todsacerdoti · Nov 27, 05:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50663089)

Others disputed the payoff: one said TLA+ specifications had not materially improved software quality, another invoked Knuth&\#x27;s warning that proved-correct code may still not do the desired thing, and a third questioned whether formal verification is feasible given the state space of real systems.
