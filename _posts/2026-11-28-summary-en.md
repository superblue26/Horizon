### [Estúdio 41’s Guaratuba House: Precast Concrete and Brise-Soleil on Brazil’s Coast](https://www.dezeen.com/2026/11/27/guaratuba-house-brazil-estudio-41/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · Dezeen Architecture · Nov 27, 18:00

**「Context」** Coastal residential work in southern Brazil has to balance intense sun, flood exposure and a client wish for close landscape views. Curitiba-based Estúdio 41’s Guaratuba House, in the seaside community of Prainha, tests that balance through a white folding brise-soleil and an exposed precast concrete frame.

**「Design response」** The 375-square-metre, two-storey house is wrapped in perforated melamine screens that filter light, reduce solar gain on glazing and open to reveal views; at night the skin reads as a lantern. Because the site has experienced storm surges, the building is lifted 70 centimetres above grade. Its precast system — six columns, four beams and 20 prestressed double-tee “π” slabs — was assembled in six hours, with the concrete left visible. The ground floor contains an open-plan kitchen, dining and living area plus service rooms and a guest bedroom, opening to a garden, barbecue terrace and pool; upstairs are four bedrooms, a family room and a bridge to a concrete terrace overlooking the sea, and the architects describe the system as simple and easily executable for the local workforce.

**「Takeaway」** For architects, the project shows how prefabrication, flood elevation and a lightweight solar screen can be combined into a legible, low-complexity residential system. It is less a digital-workflow case study than a reminder that construction method and climate response can shape architectural expression.

rss · CIC News \(加拿大工签与移民\) · Nov 27, 19:03

**「Background」** Canada’s latest measure extends temporary public-health border restrictions first introduced in May 2026, when the government used expanded executive powers under Bill C-12 to suspend immigration documents and impose arrival health requirements. The restrictions target foreign nationals residing in the Democratic Republic of the Congo \(DRC\), Uganda and South Sudan amid an Ebola outbreak that the DRC’s National Institute of Public Health reported as 12,021 confirmed cases and 5,904 deaths as of Nov. 24. Although Uganda was declared Ebola-free by the WHO on Aug. 27 and South Sudan has reported no confirmed cases, Canada has maintained and repeatedly extended the measures, which were previously due to expire on Nov. 27, 2026.

**「Policy Changes」** The federal notice published Nov. 27, 2026, extends the Ebola-related entry ban and document suspension until midnight Eastern Time on Feb. 25, 2027. Approved visas, permits and eTAs remain suspended for applicants outside Canada who were residing in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan when they applied; affected individuals are to be notified by email, and suspended documents reactivate automatically once restrictions are lifted. Foreign nationals who have been in the DRC within the past 21 days are prohibited from entering or transiting Canada, while Canadian citizens and landed permanent residents with recent travel to the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan may enter subject to a mandatory health assessment and a 21-day quarantine, or medical isolation if symptomatic. IRCC will continue processing new and existing applications from residents of the three countries, but final decisions will be withheld until the restrictions end. The order, published in the Nov. 28 Canada Gazette, follows three extensions since May 2026 and includes Quarantine Act penalties of up to $1 million, three years’ imprisonment, or both.

**「Takeaway」** For architects, BIM/AEC professionals and other skilled workers, the change has little direct effect unless they are residing in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan or have recent DRC travel; applications may still be processed, but final decisions are paused. Employers and applicants should plan for continued uncertainty rather than treat processing activity as approval. The claim comes from a Google Research blog post, and the supplied material does not include benchmark details, baseline configurations, or independent validation. It should therefore be treated as a vendor-reported result rather than a confirmed general speedup.

hackernews · ComputedCamp995 · Nov 27, 23:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50672565)

**「Background」** Transformer residual connections traditionally add each layer’s output to a running sum, which the DeepCrossAttention paper argues can dilute useful information. DCA instead lets layers learn to select and route features from earlier layers on a per-token basis, generalizing the fixed-sum design. The approach was detailed in a February 2025 arXiv paper and presented at ICML 2025 before Google Research published the current blog post.

**「Impact」** For teams working with transformer architectures, the reported result points to residual-connection design as a possible lever for reducing compute cost at comparable quality, but adoption would require reproducing the setup and checking speed and quality on the target model because no independent benchmarks are supplied.

### [Essay examines false computer-checked proofs and checker soundness bugs](https://cr.yp.to/proofs/falseccp.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new essay at cr.yp.to examines false computer-checked proofs and soundness bugs in proof checkers, focusing on how an incorrect checker could accept an invalid proof. The topic is relevant to formal-verification systems such as Lean and Metamath, where acceptance by a checker is treated as evidence of correctness. The page is an argument and analysis piece rather than a tool release or a new proof result.

hackernews · \_tk\_ · Nov 27, 15:26 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50667989)

**「Why a checked proof can be false」** A computer-checked proof is only as trustworthy as the kernel that accepts it: a soundness bug in that kernel can certify a false theorem even when the proof contains no \`sorry\` or added axiom. In 2026, Lean 4 kernel bug \#14576 \(CVE-2026-72844\) allowed construction of a checked proof of \`False\`, and the independent Rust-based checker nanoda also missed that proof because of a separate projection-verification bug.

**「Impact」** For formal-verification projects, the discussion implies that trust should rest in a verification pipeline—multiple independent checkers plus human review—rather than in any single checker&\#x27;s acceptance. A soundness bug matters most when it is the only barrier between an invalid proof and a consequential decision.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters argued that the essay understates layered verification: twotwotwo said misleading mathematicians would require bugs in multiple checkers plus a result that survives informal review, and dwheeler said Metamath&\#x27;s main databases require proofs to pass five independently created verifiers in five programming languages. gr\_norm added that known soundness bugs in long-used systems have been rare enough for most software needs, while andai raised the separate risk of proving an implementation against a mistaken specification.
